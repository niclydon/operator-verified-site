What happens when a sealed post changes

The question

Every sealed post on this site carries the same challenge, and it deserves a direct answer: the operator controls this website, so what stops him editing a sealed post afterward and leaving the badge where it was? Nothing stops the edit. A seal was never a lock. What it removes is the option of editing quietly.

What the signature covers

A seal is a signature over the exact words of a post, in their canonical form. Change one character and the post's fingerprint no longer matches the fingerprint that was signed. The original signature still exists and is still valid — for the original words, whose fingerprint was anchored on World Chain at a block time nobody can move after the fact. It simply does not describe the edited text, and no editing of the text can make it do so.

What the badge cannot know

The badge at the foot of a post is drawn from the seal record when the page is read. It never reads the page itself, so on an edited post it still shows the original seal, looking entirely normal. That is stated here rather than hidden, because it is the honest shape of the system: the badge is a claim, and a claim is only worth what the check behind it is worth. The badge always links to the check.

What the check reports

Tap the badge and the verify page fetches this post from its canonical address, recomputes the fingerprint from the words it actually finds, and compares it against the signature and the chain record. On a changed post it reports the failure by name: content-mismatch. The signature check still passes, because the signature is genuine; the content check fails, because the words are no longer the sealed words. Failures are reported one by one, by name — there are eight of them — and never collapsed into a vague invalid.

This post is the exhibit

This article was sealed exactly as first published, the passage below included. It is a promise of precisely the kind an operator might later wish he had not made, which is what makes it the right exhibit:

One promise, made here in writing: every post published on this site from the launch of the sealing system onward carries its seal from the day it appears, the founding post included, with no exceptions quietly carved out later.

The plan for this post, stated in advance: after sealing, that passage is struck through and the post is republished with no new seal. If it appears struck through as you read this, the edit has already happened — and that single visible change is the entire difference between the page you are reading and the page that was sealed. The badge below still shows the original seal. The verify page behind it reports the failure. Both are telling the truth about different things, and the difference between them is the whole design.

What still works

The original seal still verifies against the original text: the record and its chain anchor remain public, and this site's history holds both versions. Honest revision has its own path — a new seal that names the seal it supersedes, openly. What no one can do is make changed words pass as sealed words, move an anchor earlier than its block, or remove the trail without the verify page reporting that no seal was found. The seal does not stop the operator from changing his mind. It stops him from changing the record.
