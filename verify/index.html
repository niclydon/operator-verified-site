How verification works

This page explains what our projects check and what their results mean. The other checks are described below. You can try World ID here.

01 / THE ONE CLAIM

An operator registers an agent wallet in AgentBook with their verified World ID. The operator signs an attestation binding that wallet to a Discord application. A checker verifies the signature, checks the expiry, and resolves the wallet live in AgentBook. The result, stated exactly: the operator was verified as a unique human. It does not prove the human wrote any particular message, endorses any particular output, or controls the bot at this moment.

02 / HOW A CHECK RUNS
  1. The signature on the attestation recovers to the wallet it names.
  2. The attestation has not expired.
  3. A live AgentBook lookup resolves the wallet's registration.
  4. The public result shows the status, a truncated wallet address, a hash of the human identifier, and the time of the check.

Two results are kept distinct, always: not registered (the lookup resolved and found nothing) and could not be checked (the lookup itself failed). A failed lookup is never shown as a failed registration. The full identifier is resolved privately inside the check and never displayed; what a public page may show is its hash.

03 / THREE WAYS TO CHECK

The verify page

No install. You will paste an application id and read the result. Part of Fiador (human-backed attestation for Discord bots); design-stage until it ships.

The self-hosted verifier

A server admin will run the verifier under their own bot token; it is designed to grant or remove the server's “Human-backed” role. There is no central hosted bot in v1, and no invite link is coming — that is deliberate.

The served badge

Designed as an image rendered from a live check at request time, carrying its check time, and decaying visibly when a registration lapses. A copied static badge is a picture, not a proof.
